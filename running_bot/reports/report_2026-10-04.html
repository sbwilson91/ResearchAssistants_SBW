The 1.1 acute:chronic ratio sits cleanly in the productive zone and there's no injury-risk flag here — load is manageable and HRV at 98 ms with a 'balanced' status confirms the nervous system is coping. Readiness at 72/100 is moderate but functional, and sleep averaging 6.4h with a worst night of 6.1h is right on the edge of the adaptation threshold you've been fighting all year. It's not flagging as a crisis this week, but it's also not the 7–8h that would support genuine supercompensation if you were in a hard block. Worth noting: HRV is healthy and load is controlled — this combination is the clean bill of health scenario, not a masked stress signal.

VO₂ max at 54.6 ml/kg/min with a -0.3 decline over four weeks is the more interesting number. It's not alarming — a plateau-to-slight-decline post heavy racing and ultra is textbook — but it does quantify the gap to your targets clearly. Sub-1:30 half marathon typically maps to a VO₂ max of around 58 ml/kg/min, and you're sitting 3.4 points below that. The Garmin half marathon prediction of 1:34:24 (+4:24 from target) is consistent with this gap, and it's directionally honest: you ran Copenhagen at your current fitness ceiling, and that ceiling needs to be raised rather than approached again.

The 10K prediction of 42:32 is genuinely strong — 1:02 ahead of your PB — which suggests top-end speed capacity is there, and the marathon projection of 3:24:38 would be a significant PB. These numbers reflect good underlying fitness but a half marathon model that requires more sustained lactate threshold work than the current training mix is providing. The fitness is not disappearing; it's just not sharpening.

Cadence at 179.8 spm during the Barcelona intervals is right on target and consistent with your June peak — this is genuinely good news and suggests that the neuromuscular pattern is holding even without the Tuesday MRC sessions to reinforce it. The fact that this was achieved across four short but varied-effort intervals (not just one sustained threshold block) indicates the cadence habit is becoming more automatic under varied conditions.

Vertical oscillation remains stubbornly at 9.3 cm against an 8.0 cm target — unchanged from prior weeks and consistent with the pattern all year. This continues to point to posterior chain weakness as the underlying mechanism rather than a cue-driven problem: you can run with high cadence and still bounce excessively if the glutes and calves aren't generating enough horizontal drive. The calf S&C work (2×/week target) is the lever here, and this metric is the most direct feedback on whether it's being done consistently. With the race block now done and a clean training window ahead, this is actually the ideal moment to make genuine progress on vertical oscillation — volume is lower, the nervous system is fresh, and there's no race proximity forcing you to back off strength work.
