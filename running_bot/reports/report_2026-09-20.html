The acute:chronic ratio of 1.1 sits squarely in the productive zone, which is exactly where you want to be on race day — not suppressed, not overloaded. After the Backyard Ultra disruption and the September rebuild, arriving at the start line with a load ratio in range is a small but real achievement. HRV at 96ms balanced and readiness at 79/100 (HIGH) confirm the nervous system is coping well. Sleep at 6.4h average and a worst night of 5.8h is below the 6.5h adaptation threshold, but with a sleep score of 63/100 and readiness still in the high bracket, this is noise rather than signal — the body is ready.

The VO₂ max trend is the honest number to confront: 55.2 in late August, sliding to 54.4 now — a 0.8 ml/kg/min decline over three weeks. Sub-1:30 half marathon typically demands ~58 ml/kg/min, and the Garmin prediction of 1:34:21 reflects that gap — 4 minutes 21 seconds off target. That prediction is a model, not a verdict, and Garmin's HM model has historically underestimated performance when threshold work has been consistent, but it's worth naming plainly: the fitness trajectory coming into this race was disrupted by the Backyard Ultra and the incomplete September reactivation block. The sub-1:30 was always a 1-in-4 shot given the ultra sitting 3 weeks prior; the data suggests today is more likely a 1:31–1:36 day. The more meaningful number is the marathon prediction of 3:23:56 — 6:49 ahead of PB — which suggests the aerobic base built through this cycle is genuinely translating into deeper fitness, even if the HM-specific speed hasn't fully sharpened.

Cadence from the 15th's quality session measured 174.7 spm — right on target for sustained fast efforts, and consistent with the pattern seen through July and August when quality work has reliably pulled cadence into the 172–179 spm range. No regression to flag here. Vertical oscillation at 9.3cm (93mm) remains 1.3cm above the 8.0cm target — this is a persistent pattern, not a new concern, and the root cause remains posterior chain: the calves and glutes aren't yet generating enough propulsive force to reduce the bobbing. On race day, the practical implication is efficiency leakage — at 4:16/km pace, excess oscillation costs watts. The S&C work flagged since the km-37 calf failure at Copenhagen Marathon is still the mechanism that will close this gap, and it remains an open item heading into any future build.
