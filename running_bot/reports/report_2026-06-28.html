The concern is sleep: 6.2h average with a 5.3h worst night is below the threshold where adaptation gets compromised, especially for a postdoc managing demanding lab work. Not alarming yet, but watch if it's still slow next week.

Cadence at 172.4 spm across five quality intervals is solidly within the 170–180 target range and represents a genuine improvement from the 163–170 spm easy-day ceiling this athlete has historically shown. The fact that all five intervals stayed above 170 spm — with interval one hitting 175 spm at 4:10/km — suggests the neuromuscular pattern is becoming more automatic under load, not just something Sean consciously reaches for. This matters for Copenhagen: at 4:15/km half marathon pace, maintaining 172+ spm will reduce ground contact time and limit the calf recruitment overload that derailed the CPH Marathon at km 37.

Vertical oscillation at 9.3cm (93mm) remains 13mm above the 80mm target and is essentially unchanged from the known baseline. This is the persistent economy leak — at sub-1:30 HM pace over 21km, excess vertical oscillation burns energy that should be going into forward propulsion. The good news is cadence and vertical oscillation are directly linked: as cadence pushed toward 175 spm in interval one, oscillation typically drops in concert. Building the S&C work Sean has identified (posterior chain, calves) into long run finishes would start to reinforce the structural capacity to hold these mechanics when fatigued — which is precisely when they break down in races.
