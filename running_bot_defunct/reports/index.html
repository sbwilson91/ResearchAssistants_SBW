Cadence at 174 spm during threshold efforts is solidly within the 170–180 spm target range — a clean week on this metric, particularly meaningful given it was hit during a 12-minute sustained effort at Tuesday's MRC rather than a short burst. This matters because Copenhagen Half at 4:15/km for 21km is precisely the kind of sustained-effort cadence that needs to feel automatic, not forced. Vertical oscillation at 8.9cm (89mm weekly average) is still 9mm above the 8.0cm target, and this is the metric that links most directly to your known limiters. The mechanism is straightforward: excessive bounce means more energy absorbed by the calves at ground contact rather than translated into forward propulsion — and your calves were the structure that gave out at km 37 in Copenhagen Marathon. The 267ms ground contact time and 7.88% vertical ratio are consistent with this pattern. Cadence improvements during quality work are necessary but not sufficient here; the underlying fix remains posterior chain strength. Without deliberate S&C targeting the calves and glutes, the oscillation will persist regardless of cadence cues, and the calf endurance risk into a 24-hour Backyard Ultra is non-trivial.
