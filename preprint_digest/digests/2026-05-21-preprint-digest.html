From time-course expression to gene regulation: direct linear ODE inference without finite-difference approximation

Huang, X.; Ang, A.; Vasoya, A. P.; Wang, Y.; Teresa, P. | Systems Biology | 2026-05-20 | scRNA

This research addresses the critical challenge of accurately inferring gene regulatory networks from time-course expression data, which is essential for understanding how cells transition between states in various biological contexts. Existing methods often model gene expression dynamics using ordinary differential equations (ODEs) but rely on finite-difference approximations, which can introduce noise, bias, and unstable estimates. To overcome this, the authors developed a novel computational method that directly learns a linear ODE model without these approximations. Their approach formulates an optimization problem leveraging the ODE system's closed-form solution, solved efficiently via gradient descent with high-order Taylor approximations for analytical gradients. The study theoretically demonstrates an inherent, non-vanishing gap between their exact solution and those derived from finite-difference approximations, underscoring the superior accuracy and theoretical advantages of their direct method for gene regulation inference.
