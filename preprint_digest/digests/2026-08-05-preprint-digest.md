### [Meta-analyses of the NEPTUNE dataset related to high and low risk FSGS and cellular APOL1 models identifies Calcium signalling, mTOR signaling and inflammation-associated pathways as operative in APOL1-mediated kidney disease.](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.30.741742)
**Wruck, W.; Thimm, C.; Adjaye, J.** | Molecular Biology | 2026-07-31 | `organoid`

> This study investigated how specific variants of the APOL1 gene, known to increase kidney disease risk but offer protection against sleeping sickness, affect kidney cells. Researchers analyzed gene activity in kidney biopsies from patients with high-risk and low-risk APOL1 variants, and also used cell models. They found distinct patterns of gene activity, with some genes related to calcium and mTOR signaling being more active, and others involved in inflammation and immune responses being less active in high-risk individuals. These findings suggest that APOL1 risk variants may alter kidney cell function through these specific pathways, and the study identified potential drug candidates, like an mTOR inhibitor, that could reverse some of these changes.


### [Single-cell lineage tracing of human neuromesoderm organoids reveals TBX6-mediated posterior mesoderm fate diversification](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.29.741448)
**Liao, Y.; Chen, C.; Li, M.; Wang, C.; Wang, L.; Zhu, M.; Yao, Y.; Peng, G.** | Developmental Biology | 2026-07-30 | `organoid`

> This study investigated the developmental pathways of human neuromesodermal progenitors (NMPs), which are crucial for forming the posterior part of the body. Researchers used a sophisticated CRISPR/Cas9 system for single-cell lineage tracing combined with gene expression and accessibility profiling in lab-grown human organoids. They discovered that intermediate and paraxial mesoderm arise from a common precursor after NMPs, while lateral plate mesoderm separates earlier. The findings also show that a gene called TBX6 is essential for the development of intermediate mesoderm and somites, and its absence halts the maturation of these tissues. This research provides a detailed map of human NMP development, improving our understanding of how the trunk forms and the causes of certain birth defects.

> This study investigated how early brain development is affected in juvenile Huntington's disease (JOHD). Researchers used lab-grown organoids derived from stem cells of JOHD patients to model the interactions between different brain regions. They discovered that JOHD organoids showed abnormal growth patterns and developmental delays, such as stalled progenitor cell growth and delayed neuron maturation. ### [Therapeutic acetaminophen exposure does not measurably alter human cortical organoid development](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.07.681041)
**Trovo, L.; Kucera, G.; Gomez, G.; de Souza, J. S.; Muotri, A. R.; Iakoucheva, L. M.** | Neuroscience | 2026-07-29 | `organoid`

> This study investigated how acetaminophen, a common pain reliever used during pregnancy, affects the development of the human brain before birth. Researchers used lab-grown human brain organoids derived from stem cells and exposed them to different levels of acetaminophen. They found that acetaminophen did not significantly alter the organoids' growth, cell death, or the development of neurons and synapses. While some minor gene expression changes were observed at the highest dose, overall, the organoids' structure and function appeared unaffected by the exposure. These findings suggest that acetaminophen may not have a substantial impact on early human brain development, though further research is needed.
