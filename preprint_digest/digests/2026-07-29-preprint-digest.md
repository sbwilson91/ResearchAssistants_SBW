## Brain

### [Linguistic structure and probability are jointly encoded in high gamma power](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739808)
**Slaats, S.; Hervais-Adelman, A.** | Neuroscience | 2026-07-22 | `large language model`

> This study investigated how the brain combines words into phrases and sentences during speech comprehension. Researchers analyzed brain activity from intracranial recordings using advanced statistical methods. They found that brain activity, specifically high gamma power, reflects both the likelihood of words appearing together and the grammatical structure of sentences. The findings suggest that building sentence structure involves communication between different brain regions and that the brain might prioritize either word meaning or grammatical structure depending on the direction of information flow.

## Liver

### [IL-32 is a stress-responsive node locked in a noncanonical NF-κB inflammatory loop during MASLD to HCC transition](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739717)
**Zhao, L. N.; Kaldis, P.; Andersen, J.** | Cancer Biology | 2026-07-22 | `spatial transcriptomics`

> This study investigated why Interleukin-32 (IL-32) is high in liver cancer but protective against fatty liver disease. Researchers analyzed patient data using transcriptomics, metabolomics, and single-cell techniques. They discovered that a specific signaling pathway (noncanonical NF-κB) becomes the main driver of IL-32 in cancer, creating a self-sustaining inflammation loop. This switch, facilitated by a factor called FOXO1, disconnects IL-32's link to lipid metabolism, making cancer cells more susceptible to inflammation.


### [A TLS11a-decorated ionizable lipid nanoparticle platform and a multilevel-validated CRISPR LDLR-knockout HepG2 model for hepatocyte-preferential mRNA delivery](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739738)
**Hussain, I.; Kholaif, N.; Alsultan, R.; Eltahir, R.; Alajlan, H.; Mir, T. A.; Salma, J.; Ur Rehman, F.; Alazami, A. M.; ...** | Bioengineering | 2026-07-22 | `CRISPR`

> This study investigated a new way to deliver CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing tools specifically to liver cells, bypassing a common uptake pathway that can limit effectiveness. Researchers created nanoparticles coated with a special molecule (aptamer) that targets liver cells directly. These aptamer-coated nanoparticles successfully delivered the gene editing components to liver cells more efficiently and with greater selectivity compared to standard methods. This development could improve the precision and success of gene editing therapies for liver diseases, especially in cases where the usual delivery route is compromised.

## General

### [The carbon-nitrogen metabolism gene E1OGDH1 influences maize root system architecture and nitrogen plasticity](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739812)
**Cho, M.; Liu, Z.; Luebbert, C.; Ziegler, G.; Thiruppathi, D.; Tiskevich, C.; Liu, H.; Yang, B.; Baxter, I.; Moose, S. P....** | Plant Biology | 2026-07-22 | `CRISPR`

> This study investigated how root structure influences a plant's ability to absorb nitrogen from the soil, aiming to improve crop yields and reduce fertilizer waste. Researchers analyzed root systems from a specially bred corn population and used genetic analysis to pinpoint a gene, E1OGDH1, linked to nitrogen uptake. They found that variations in this gene, caused by differences in its regulatory region, affect how much nitrogen the plant can absorb. This discovery suggests that targeting E1OGDH1 could be a way to breed crops with better nitrogen efficiency.


### [CRISPR-Cas9 Induced Knockout of BEL5 in Tetraploid Potato: Optimized Methodology via Repeated de novo Regeneration and Impact on Tuberization](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739726)
**Zounkova, A.; Chirivi, D.; Pribylova, A.; Martignago, D.; Myslivcova, J.; Masek, T.; Fischer, L.; Betti, C.; Fornara, F....** | Plant Biology | 2026-07-22 | `CRISPR`

> This study aimed to overcome challenges in editing the complex genome of tetraploid potatoes using CRISPR-Cas9. Researchers used Agrobacterium-mediated transformation and repeated regeneration to achieve a complete knockout of the BEL5 gene, which is important for tuber formation. They found that plants without BEL5 showed a delay in starting to form tubers but produced similar overall yields. This work suggests BEL5 influences the timing of tuber initiation but is not essential for tuber development in modern potatoes, and it also provides a method for efficient gene editing in this crop.


### [Activity and specificity trade-offs in adenine base editors](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.20.739671)
**Lukarska, M.; Oltrogge, L. M.; Nisonoff, H.; Long, Y.; Terrace, C. I.; Busia, A.; Aquino, C.; Kim, S. E.; Listgarten, J....** | Molecular Biology | 2026-07-22 | `CRISPR`

> This study investigated how to improve the precision of adenine base editors (ABEs), a gene-editing tool. Researchers used machine learning to design and test thousands of ABE variants, measuring their on-target and off-target editing in bacteria and mammalian cells. They discovered that the editing efficiency of ABEs is primarily determined by a single factor, intrinsic deaminase activity, rather than specialization in other areas like sequence context or editing window. This finding implies that current ABE designs have inherent limitations in balancing activity and specificity, suggesting that significant architectural changes are needed for future improvements.


### [Generation of lachrymatory factor synthase-suppressed onion (Allium cepa L.) by Agrobacterium-mediated gene transfer for CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.21.739049)
**Tamaru, S.; Imai, S.; Watanabe, S.; Ikegai, T.; Kondo, S.; Igawa, T.; Kamoi, T.** | Plant Biology | 2026-07-22 | `CRISPR`

> This study aimed to reduce the tear-inducing compounds in onions by genetically modifying the enzyme responsible for their production. Researchers used CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing technology to target and alter the lachrymatory factor synthase (LFS) gene in onion plants. They successfully created edited plants with lower LFS enzyme activity, indicating a reduction in tear-inducing compounds. While this demonstrated the feasibility of editing specific onion traits, the edited plants showed significant growth problems and could not produce seeds, suggesting further research is needed to achieve the goal of producing "tear-free" onions.
