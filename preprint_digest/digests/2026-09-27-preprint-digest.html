Type I and Type III Interferons Differentially Shape Antiviral Defense and Epithelial Integrity at the Choroid Plexus

Steppe, J. T.; Heckenberg, E.; Hale, C.; Coyne, C. | Immunology | 2026-09-25 | organoid

This study investigated how the choroid plexus, a barrier in the brain, responds to echovirus infection. Researchers used human organoids and mouse models to observe the infection process and the body's defense mechanisms. They found that the choroid plexus cells were a primary target for the virus, and while type I interferons helped fight the infection, type III interferons, though not controlling the virus, caused damage to the choroid plexus. This damage led to long-term neurological problems like motor deficits and enlarged ventricles, suggesting type III interferons play a role in lasting brain injury after viral infections.
