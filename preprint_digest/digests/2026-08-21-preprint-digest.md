### [Loss of nephronophthisis-associated nephrocystin-1 impairs DNA damage repair in kidney organoids](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.06.09.658557)
**Sendino Garvi, E.; Biermans, S.; Knoers, N. N. V. A. M.; van Eerde, A. A. M.; Masereeuw, R.; Janssen, M. J.; Slaats, G. ...** | Molecular Biology | 2026-08-19 | `organoid`

> This study investigated why mutations in the NPHP1 gene cause nephronophthisis, a kidney disease leading to kidney failure. Researchers created kidney organoids from human stem cells, either with normal NPHP1 or with NPHP1 removed. They found that without NPHP1, the organoids developed faulty kidney structures and lost important kidney cells. Crucially, these NPHP1-deficient organoids struggled to repair DNA damage and showed signs of aging and scarring. The findings suggest that nephrocystin-1, the protein produced by NPHP1, plays a direct role in DNA repair within kidney cells.

### [A New aspect of the pathology of brain tuberculosis: Mycobacterium tuberculosis infects and alters human neural progenitor cells](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.16.745136)
**Priyathilaka, T. T.; Herbath, M.; Kumar, M.; Laaker, C. J.; Schwartz, M. P.; Lebakken, C.; Fabry, Z.; Sandor, M.** | Immunology | 2026-08-20 | `organoid`

> This study investigated how *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (Mtb) infects the brain by using human neural organoids. Researchers found that a specific type of brain cell, neural progenitor cells (NPCs), directly take up the bacteria through receptors on their surface. Once inside, the bacteria are found in different cellular compartments and can multiply, triggering an immune response from the infected NPCs. This infection also leads to increased cell death and hinders the development of new brain cells, though some of these effects can be reduced by blocking a specific immune signal. These findings suggest that these organoid models are a promising new way to study brain tuberculosis and its impact on brain cells.

### [Mutation-induced phenotypic plasticity drives formation of a pro-metastatic tumour-intrinsic niche](https://doi.org/10.1101/2022.12.22.521159)
**Bugter, J. M.; El Bouazzaoui, L.; Kucukkose, E.; Mills, M. L.; Hong, Y.; Sprangers, J.; Jordens, I.; Kokkinidi, D.; Plug...** | Cancer Biology | 2026-08-20 | `organoid`

> This study investigates how colorectal cancer (CRC) cells become more adaptable and diverse, leading to metastasis and poorer patient outcomes, particularly in BRAF-mutant CRC. Researchers used patient-derived organoids, gene editing, and mouse models to explore this. They discovered that mutations in RNF43 create a non-dividing cell type, called a tumor-intrinsic niche cell (TINC), which allows the cancer to produce its own growth factors. These TINCs are found in aggressive human CRC and are crucial for the cancer to spread to distant organs, suggesting that blocking their formation could prevent metastasis.
