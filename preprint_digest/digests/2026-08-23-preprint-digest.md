### [Mutagenic bypass of uracil-derived abasic sites underlies the SBS17 mutational signature](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.21.746158)

> This study investigated the cause of a common type of DNA mutation (SBS17) found in stomach and esophageal cancers, which previously had no clear explanation. Researchers used genetic experiments in cell cultures and organoids, along with whole-genome sequencing, to uncover the mechanism. They found that SBS17 mutations are not caused by oxidative damage as previously thought, but rather by errors during DNA replication where a molecule called dUTP is incorrectly inserted. This is then repaired in a way that leads to mutations, and this process is responsible for both natural mutations and those caused by chemotherapy. Understanding this mechanism is significant because it explains a major source of mutations in these cancers and could lead to new ways to prevent cancer progression and drug resistance.

> This study investigates whether foundation models for plant single-cell transcriptomics can learn generalizable representations of cellular states that perform well across different experiments. The researchers developed a foundation model for Arabidopsis thaliana called scAraFM and tested its performance using various evaluation methods, including splitting data from a single experiment and transferring knowledge between different experiments. They discovered that evaluating models on data from the same experiment can overestimate their true performance, and that preserving gene identity in the model's representations is important. This highlights the importance of careful evaluation design when assessing the capabilities of these models.
