### [Neural rosettes emerge from an interconnected tubular network in human cortical organoids](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.16.767152)
**Trovo, L.; Gomez, G.; Einhorn, A.; Muotri, A. R.; Iakoucheva, L. M.** | Neuroscience | 2026-11-18 | `organoid`

> This research investigates how neural rosettes, structures found in lab-grown brain organoids, actually form. Scientists used fluorescent nanoplastics to trace the development of these rosettes, observing how they arise from a continuous, interconnected tubular network. They found that this network forms through processes similar to how the neural tube develops in a real brain, involving folding and internalization. This discovery offers a new understanding of early brain development and suggests brain organoids could be useful for studying birth defects affecting the neural tube.

> This research investigated how simulated microgravity affects different human tissues. Scientists used rotating-wall vessels to expose neural, cardiac, and tonsil organoids to a lack of mechanical load for 24 hours. They found that these organoids showed tissue-specific changes in gene activity, similar to what has been observed in actual spaceflight.
