**CUELLA MARTIN, C.; NGUYEN, T. T.; GEISTLICH, K.; BERTIN, A.; GUYOT, B.; DELAY, E.; DALVERNY, C.; BOISSARD, A.; HENRY, C....** | Cancer Biology | 2026-08-12 | `organoid`

> This study investigated how tunneling nanotubes (TNTs) contribute to the very beginning of breast cancer development. Researchers used human breast cells and organoids to observe TNT formation during early cell changes. They found that transformed cells create more and longer TNTs, which connect to normal cells and transfer molecular signals. These signals alter the normal cells' gene expression and protein makeup, leading to changes like the ability to grow without being attached to a surface, a key step in cancer progression. This suggests TNTs play a crucial role in initiating cancer by influencing normal cells.

> This study addresses the problem of inaccurate gene expression measurements in spatial transcriptomics caused by mRNA diffusion between locations. The researchers developed a new computational framework called CLEAR-ST, which uses a denoising autoencoder and a physics-informed model to correct for this contamination. Their findings show that out-of-tissue signals are linked to nearby gene expression and that diffusion patterns can be consistent across genes. This work is significant because CLEAR-ST improves the accuracy of spatial transcriptomics data, leading to better identification of biological regions and stronger detection of spatial gene patterns.
