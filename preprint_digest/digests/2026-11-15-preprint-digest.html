Evaluation design shapes estimates of cross-donor generalization in single-cell classification of cell-type

Chen, X.; Li, J.; Battle, A. | Bioinformatics | 2026-11-10 | scRNA

This study investigated how different ways of testing cell-type classifiers affect their ability to generalize to new individuals. Researchers found that randomly splitting cells for testing, instead of holding out entire donors, consistently overestimated classifier performance. This overestimation was observed even after accounting for technical variations and using advanced statistical models. The findings suggest that holding out donors is crucial for accurately assessing how well classifiers will work on data from new people, especially when the classification task is challenging.

The scientists engineered a new type of T-cell therapy using a "chimeric switch receptor" (CSR) that specifically targets PD-L1. This study investigated how problems with mitochondria, the powerhouses of cells, might lead to brain diseases. Researchers used mice and lab-grown human brain models that mimicked a specific mitochondrial disorder. They discovered that while the brain cells tried to fight off viruses, this response didn't help the disease. Instead, they found a protein called PKR, which normally detects viral RNA, was actually causing damage to the mitochondria and brain cells. Blocking PKR improved the disease symptoms and survival, even though it didn't fix the original mitochondrial problem. This suggests that PKR's role in sensing RNA is a key factor in this type of brain disease and that targeting PKR could be a potential treatment.
