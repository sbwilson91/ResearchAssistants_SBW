ST3GAL3 loss-of-function disrupts synaptic integrity and excitatory/inhibitory cortical dynamics

Diouf, D.; Tsounis, D. L.; Pishva, E.; Vanmierlo, T.; van den Hove, D.; Lesch, K.-P. | Neuroscience | 2026-06-25 | CRISPR

This study investigated how the gene ST3GAL3 influences the balance between excitatory and inhibitory signals in brain synapses, which is often disrupted in neurodevelopmental disorders. Researchers used human stem cells, creating versions with and without the ST3GAL3 gene, and then differentiated them into brain cells. They found that cells lacking ST3GAL3 showed abnormal electrical activity patterns, with longer and more variable bursts of signals. Furthermore, the study revealed that the absence of ST3GAL3 led to reduced expression of genes important for learning, memory, and the function of excitatory and inhibitory synapses. This suggests ST3GAL3 plays a crucial role in maintaining proper synaptic function and could be a target for understanding and treating neurodevelopmental conditions.

This study investigated how the herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) affects the metabolism of nerve cells, specifically in relation to Alzheimer's disease. Researchers found that HSV-1 hijacks the cell's peroxisomes, which are involved in lipid metabolism, to boost its own replication.
