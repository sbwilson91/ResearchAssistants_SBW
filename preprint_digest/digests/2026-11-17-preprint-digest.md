### [Distinct calcium, sodium, and water programs characterize mouse and human connecting tubule](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.13.766571)

> This study investigated the function of the kidney connecting tubule, a short but important segment for regulating water and electrolyte balance. Researchers used single nucleus RNA sequencing on enriched mouse kidney connecting tubule cells to identify distinct cell types. They discovered three groups of cells within the connecting tubule, with one group specialized for calcium transport and another for water transport, and an intermediate group. The findings indicate that these specialized cells separate calcium and water transport, preventing calcium buildup, and also restrict aldosterone's effects to specific areas to help maintain sodium balance.


### [Evaluation design shapes estimates of cross-donor generalization in single-cell classification of cell-type](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.26.761949)
**Chen, X.; Li, J.; Battle, A.** | Bioinformatics | 2026-11-10 | `scRNA`

> This study investigated how different ways of testing cell-type classifiers affect how well they work on new individuals. The researchers found that splitting data randomly by cell, instead of by donor, consistently overestimated classifier performance. This overestimation was observed even after accounting for technical variations and using advanced statistical models. The findings suggest that holding out entire donors for testing is crucial for accurately assessing how well classifiers generalize to new individuals, especially when the classification task is challenging.
