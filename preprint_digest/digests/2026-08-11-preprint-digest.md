### [Plaque-associated oligodendrocyte proteostatic failure underlies myelin loss in Alzheimers disease](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.08.737318)
**Shin, J.; Joshi, N.; Miller, B.; Vellarikkal, S.; Huang, J.; Wu, F.; Cui, Y.; Murali, A.; Chason, J.; Campbell, C.; Chu,...** | Neuroscience | 2026-08-06 | `organoid`

> This research investigates why myelin, crucial for brain cell communication, is lost early in Alzheimer's disease. Scientists used human stem cells to create brain organoids that could produce mature myelin-producing cells. When they introduced Alzheimer's-related mutations into these organoids, they observed myelin damage and identified that oligodendrocytes near amyloid plaques showed problems with calcium signaling, immune responses, and breaking down proteins. The study found that myelin protein levels decreased because the proteins were being degraded, not due to a lack of production, and this degradation could be reversed by blocking protein breakdown. These findings suggest that a failure in protein management within oligodendrocytes, triggered by amyloid plaques, is a key reason for myelin loss in Alzheimer's and could be a target for future treatments.
