This study investigated how specific variants of the APOL1 gene, known to increase kidney disease risk but offer protection against sleeping sickness, affect kidney cells. Researchers analyzed gene activity in kidney biopsies from patients with high-risk and low-risk APOL1 variants, and also used cell models. Single-cell lineage tracing of human neuromesoderm organoids reveals TBX6-mediated posterior mesoderm fate diversification

Liao, Y.; Chen, C.; Li, M.; Wang, C.; Wang, L.; Zhu, M.; Yao, Y.; Peng, G. | Developmental Biology | 2026-07-30 | organoid

This study investigated the developmental pathways of human neuromesodermal progenitors (NMPs), which are crucial for forming the posterior part of the body. Researchers used a sophisticated CRISPR/Cas9 system for single-cell lineage tracing combined with gene expression and accessibility profiling in lab-grown human organoids. They discovered that intermediate and paraxial mesoderm arise from a common precursor after NMPs, while lateral plate mesoderm separates earlier. The findings also show that a gene called TBX6 is essential for the development of intermediate mesoderm and somites, and its absence halts the maturation of these tissues. This research provides a detailed map of human NMP development, improving our understanding of how the trunk forms and the causes of certain birth defects.

This study investigated how acetaminophen, a common pain reliever used during pregnancy, affects the development of the human brain before birth. Researchers used lab-grown human brain organoids derived from stem cells and exposed them to different levels of acetaminophen. They found that acetaminophen did not significantly alter the organoids' growth, cell death, or the development of neurons and synapses.
