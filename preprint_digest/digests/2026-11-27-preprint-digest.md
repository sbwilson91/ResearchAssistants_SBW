> This study investigated the effectiveness of single-cell foundation models (FMs) for various biological tasks. Researchers compared five existing FMs and four newer models against simpler methods using diverse public datasets. The FMs showed the greatest benefit when predicting cell types across different species, outperforming baselines in cross-species transfer. While FMs demonstrated some ability to predict the direction of cellular responses to perturbations and infer gene regulatory networks, their performance within a species and for predicting gene deletions was less impressive compared to simpler methods.

### [NAD(P)H-FLIM reveals spatial and temporal epithelial metabolic heterogeneity in live intestinal organoids](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.25.769250)
**Zhou, H.; Okkelman, I. A.; Lei, L.; De Kinder, A.; Vereecke, L.; Lambrecht, B. N.; Jalink, K.; Dmitriev, R. I.** | Cell Biology | 2026-11-27 | `organoid`

> This study investigated how individual cells within tissues utilize energy and how this relates to their specific roles. Researchers used a technique called NAD(P)H fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy (FLIM) on living organoids, which allowed them to observe metabolic activity in individual cells without needing labels. They found that the growth environment and position within the organoid significantly impacted cellular energy use, with a clear gradient observed. Specifically, enterocytes, a type of intestinal cell, showed the highest levels of NAD(P)H, indicating robust metabolic activity and higher ATP production compared to stem cells. This work provides a new way to understand cell-specific metabolism in complex tissues and its link to cell function.

## Heart

### [A Single-Cell Transcriptomic Atlas of Human Heart Organoids across Differentiation Protocols](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.25.769101)
**Ouologuem, S.; Dakovic, V.; Zengerle, S.; Geissner, J.; Dorn, T.; Meier, A. B.; Drakhlis, L.; Makra, C.; Sima, L.; Xourg...** | Bioinformatics | 2026-11-26 | `organoid`

> This study aimed to understand how different methods for creating human heart organoids affect their development and similarity to real human hearts. Researchers analyzed a large collection of single-cell data from various heart organoid experiments to create a comprehensive reference map. They found that while organoids can accurately mimic early fetal heart development, achieving mature cell types remains a challenge. The work also identified key cell types like epicardial and vascular cells, highlighted differences in how various protocols balance cell diversity and maturity, and pointed to signaling pathways that are not well-represented in current models, offering a valuable tool for improving future heart organoid research.
