Meta-analyses of the NEPTUNE dataset related to high and low risk FSGS and cellular APOL1 models identifies Calcium signalling, mTOR signaling and inflammation-associated pathways as operative in APOL1-mediated kidney disease.

Wruck, W.; Thimm, C.; Adjaye, J. | Molecular Biology | 2026-07-31 | organoid

This study investigated how specific variants of the APOL1 gene, known to increase kidney disease risk but offer protection against sleeping sickness, affect gene activity in the kidneys. Researchers analyzed gene expression data from kidney biopsies of patients with high-risk (HR) and low-risk (LR) APOL1 variants, and compared this with various cell models. They found distinct patterns of gene activity, with certain genes related to calcium and mTOR signaling being more active in HR variants, while genes involved in inflammation and immune responses were less active. Single-cell lineage tracing of human neuromesoderm organoids reveals TBX6-mediated posterior mesoderm fate diversification

Liao, Y.; Chen, C.; Li, M.; Wang, C.; Wang, L.; Zhu, M.; Yao, Y.; Peng, G. | Developmental Biology | 2026-07-30 | organoid

This research investigates how human neuromesodermal progenitors (NMPs) develop into different cell types that form the posterior part of the body. Scientists used a sophisticated CRISPR/Cas9 system to track individual cell lineages and combined this with detailed molecular profiling (scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq) in lab-grown human organoids. They discovered that intermediate and paraxial mesoderm arise from a common precursor after NMPs, while lateral plate mesoderm separates earlier. The study also found that the gene TBX6 is crucial for the development of intermediate mesoderm and somites, and its absence prevents the maturation of these tissues. This work offers a detailed map of human NMP development, improving our understanding of how the spine and kidneys form and the causes of related birth defects.

This study investigated how acetaminophen, a common pain reliever used during pregnancy, affects the development of the human brain before birth. Researchers used lab-grown human brain organoids, which mimic early brain development, and exposed them to different levels of acetaminophen. They found that acetaminophen did not significantly alter the organoids' growth, cell survival, or the development of neurons and their connections. This study investigated how the movement of mRNA molecules changes as human stem cells differentiate into specific cell types.
