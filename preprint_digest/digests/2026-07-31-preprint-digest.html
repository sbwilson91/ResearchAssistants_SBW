Kruppel-like factors KLF5 & KLF8 emerge as master transcriptional regulators of Alzheimers disease, as revealed on cell fate regulomes in human brain organoids

Aubert, A.; Comby, A.-C.; Bramoulle, A.; Mendoza-Ferri, M.-G.; Azzolin, P.; Li, H.; Moussy, A.; Das, S.; Colombo, B. M.;... | Systems Biology | 2026-07-24 | organoid

This study investigated which key proteins, called transcription factors, control the changes in gene activity seen in Alzheimer's disease (AD). Researchers used lab-grown human brain organoids with genetic mutations linked to AD and analyzed their gene activity over time using advanced transcriptomics. They identified 110 specific transcription factors involved in AD progression, finding that two factors, KLF5 and KLF8, appear to directly influence many of these. The study also showed that these identified factors are also active in human AD patients, suggesting their importance beyond specific genetic mutations. Ultimately, this work maps out the regulatory network driving AD and points to these transcription factors as potential targets for new treatments.

Mid-zone hepatocytes trade proliferation for survival via Atf4-Chop axis in early acute liver injury

Zhu, Y.; Deng, C.; Chen, B.; He, J.; Liu, Y.; Lei, S.; Lu, W.; Peng, C.; Shan, Z. | Pharmacology And Toxicology | 2026-07-24 | spatial transcriptomics

This study investigated how liver cells (hepatocytes) change before they start multiplying to repair liver damage. Researchers observed that in the early stages of acetaminophen-induced liver injury, hepatocytes temporarily stop dividing, especially those in the mid-zone of the liver. Using advanced spatial transcriptomics and other experiments, they found that a specific stress-response pathway involving Atf4-Chop in mid-zone hepatocytes leads to the suppression of cell division through a protein called Btg2. This suggests that mid-zone liver cells prioritize adapting to stress over immediate repair, which helps maintain their ability to regenerate later.

Imprinted regulatory networks reveal the molecular cross-talk between paternal and maternal genomes in the endosperm of Arabidopsis arenosa

Budinsky, T.; Kovacik, M.; Cermak, V.; Pribylova, A.; Salony, S.; Iltas, O.; Pecinka, A.; Lafon Placette, C. | Plant Biology | 2026-07-24 | epigenetics

This study investigated how imprinted genes, which are expressed differently depending on their parental origin, function within complex gene networks to influence seed development. Researchers analyzed the imprinted genes in *Arabidopsis arenosa* and used gene regulatory network analysis to understand their interactions. They found that imprinting primarily affects specific biological pathways, suggesting these are crucial for processes sensitive to gene dosage and potentially involved in parental conflict. The findings indicate that imprinted genes can act as central "hub" genes, with paternal regulators specifically targeting paternally expressed genes, while maternal regulators target both paternal and maternal genes, suggesting a dynamic of parental influence and buffering. This research sheds light on how genomic imprinting can mediate parental conflict and highlights the importance of coordinated parental gene expression for proper seed development.
