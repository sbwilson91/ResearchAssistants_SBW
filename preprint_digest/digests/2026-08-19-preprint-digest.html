This study investigated how genetic mutations cause microcephaly, a condition where a child's brain is significantly smaller than normal. Researchers found that a gene called Bx42 is crucial for this process, using human brain organoids and fruit fly models. They discovered that when Bx42 is absent, neural stem cells don't divide enough early in development, leading to a smaller brain.
