p53 knockout drives stage-specific long non-coding RNA reprogramming during human neural differentiation and glioma organoid tumorigenesis.

Stasi, F.; Manzari, A. R.; Siciliano, V.; Binda, E.; Pazienza, V.; Brunetti, V.; De Vittorio, M. | Cancer Biology | 2026-09-18 | organoid

This study investigated how the absence of the p53 gene affects the expression of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) during the early development of brain tumors. Researchers used human stem cells with a disabled p53 gene, differentiating them into brain cells and organoids, and then analyzed their RNA. They found that losing p53 significantly altered lncRNA patterns as brain cells developed, with a notable shift occurring in later-stage cells that resembled some aspects of glioblastoma. Specifically, five lncRNAs were found to be increased in both p53-deficient brain cells and glioblastoma cells, and some of these appear to be newly identified as potentially linked to glioma. This work suggests that p53 loss triggers changes in lncRNAs that may contribute to the development of brain tumors.

This research aimed to develop a better way to study multiple sclerosis (MS) and test treatments. This model was also able to distinguish between patients who responded to different MS medications and those who did not. This new platform could help in developing personalized treatments for MS by allowing doctors to test how individual patients might respond to therapies before administering them.
