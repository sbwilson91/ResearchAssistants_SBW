## Brain

### [Coupled transcriptomic divergence establishes a human-specific synaptic glial precursor state](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.19.745872)
**Sheu, X. D.; Yamauchi, Y. Y.; Amano, R.; Nakano, Y.; Yoshino, J.; Suzuki, I. K.** | Genomics | 2026-09-08 | `organoid`

> This study investigated how the human brain evolved its unique complexity compared to other mammals. Researchers compared gene activity during brain development across humans, macaques, mice, and ferrets, tracking how cells differentiate over time. They discovered that genes changing in humans tend to alter their timing and role in cell development together. Specifically, a network of genes related to synapses was found to be unusually active in early human oligodendrocyte precursor cells, a state not seen in other primates. This suggests that these specialized cells, by interacting with other developing brain cells through signaling, created new developmental conditions that drove human brain evolution.

## Intestine

### [Patient-Derived Organoids as a Model to Understand Tumor Microenvironment-Driven Nano-Bio Interactions - A Framework Towards Improved Nanomedicine Translation](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.09.03.749195)
**Resmi, M. P.; Chakrabarti, J.; Pond, K.; Ganesh, S.** | Bioengineering | 2026-09-07 | `organoid`

> This research addresses why nanomedicines that work in lab dishes often fail in patients, specifically focusing on how nanoparticles change in the tumor environment. The scientists used patient-derived organoids, which mimic tumors, to study how graphene and silicon quantum dots interact with cells. They found that standard cell cultures overestimate how much nanoparticles are taken up by cells and that proteins in the tumor environment direct nanoparticles to specific cell types. By engineering the nanoparticle's protein coating with tumor-specific proteins, they significantly improved delivery to drug-resistant cancer cells. The study also revealed that different cell types in the tumor take up nanoparticles through different mechanisms, affecting whether the nanoparticles are broken down or not.
