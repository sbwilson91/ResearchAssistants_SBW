### [Regulation of FGF/MAPK signaling at the primary cilium base controls the proliferation-neurogenesis balance in human cerebellar organoids](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.04.14.648790)
**Brunetti, L.; Wiegering, A.; Anselme, I.; Vu, M. N.; Pollara, L.; Grappin, M.; Catala, M.; Saulnier, O.; Antoniewski, C....** | Developmental Biology | 2026-10-31 | `organoid`

> This study investigated how problems with primary cilia, which are important for cell signaling during development, affect human brain development, specifically the cerebellum. Researchers used human cerebellar organoids with genetic mutations linked to a condition called Joubert syndrome to study this. They found that these mutated organoids had fewer key nerve cells (Purkinje cells) and problems with how new nerve cells were made, along with overactive FGF/MAPK signaling. Importantly, blocking this signaling pathway helped fix the cell production issues and the lack of Purkinje cells, even though the cilia themselves remained abnormal. This suggests that the primary cilium plays a crucial role in controlling signaling pathways that are essential for proper cerebellar development.
