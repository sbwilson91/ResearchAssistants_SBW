Egilmezer, E.; Rawlinson, W.; Foster, C. S. P. | Microbiology | 2026-09-03 | organoid

This study investigated how different antiviral drugs affect human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) infection in a model of the human brain. Researchers infected brain organoids with HCMV and treated them with four different antiviral drugs: aciclovir, ganciclovir, letermovir, and maribavir. They found that letermovir was the most effective at reducing viral RNA, followed by ganciclovir, maribavir, and then aciclovir. While all drugs reduced viral load, letermovir significantly altered host gene expression related to metabolism, whereas other drugs had less impact on the host's genetic activity. This research helps understand which antivirals might be best for treating HCMV-related neurological damage by assessing their impact on both the virus and the host brain cells.

Resmi, M. P.; Chakrabarti, J.; Pond, K.; Ganesh, S. | Bioengineering | 2026-09-07 | organoid

This study investigated why nanomedicines that work in lab dishes often fail in patients, specifically focusing on how nanoparticles change in the tumor environment. Researchers used patient-derived organoids, which better mimic tumors than standard cell cultures, to study how graphene and silicon quantum dots interact with cells. They found that standard cell cultures overestimate how much nanoparticles are taken up by cells and that proteins in the tumor environment direct nanoparticles to specific cell types.
