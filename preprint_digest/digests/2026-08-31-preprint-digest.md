### [Oligodendrocyte Enriched Brain Organoids Reveal Impaired Oligodendroglial Maturation and Altered Neural Network Activity in Down Syndrome](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.27.747500)
**Marti, M. B.; Morrison, S. D.; Al-mhanawi, B.; Noakes, P. G.; Wolvetang, E. J.; Shaker, M. R.** | Cell Biology | 2026-08-28 | `organoid`

> This study investigated how having an extra copy of chromosome 21 (trisomy 21), which causes Down syndrome, affects early brain development. Researchers used lab-grown brain organoids derived from cells of individuals with and without Down syndrome to compare their development. They found that trisomy 21 organoids showed delayed growth, fewer and less mature oligodendrocytes (cells that form myelin), and reduced myelination, along with more astrocytes and slower neuronal maturation. These cellular changes led to abnormal brain activity, with increased spontaneous network activity but impaired responses to stimulation. The findings suggest that trisomy 21 disrupts early brain cell development, particularly impacting the formation of white matter and neural circuits, and highlight the usefulness of these organoids for studying these developmental issues.
