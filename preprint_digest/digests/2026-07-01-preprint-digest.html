Spinal injury induces a stem cell-like progenitor state that promotes regenerative neurogenesis via clcf1 in zebrafish.

This study investigated whether zebrafish spinal cord regeneration after injury uses the same processes as normal development or employs unique mechanisms. Researchers used single-cell RNA sequencing and in vivo experiments to identify a specific "injury-induced ERG" (iiERG) cell state in larval zebrafish. They discovered that this iiERG state, characterized by stemness factors like lin28a, appears to preserve stem cells rather than directly generating neurons. Gene expression identifies regional central nervous system vulnerability to heat

Pagni, S.; Bouchama, A.; Sisodiya, S. | Neuroscience | 2026-06-26 | organoid

This study investigated why Purkinje neurons in the cerebellum are particularly vulnerable to heatstroke. Researchers analyzed gene activity from human heat exposure studies, heatstroke patients, and brain models to find common responses to heat stress. They discovered that heat stress generally reduces gene activity related to building and energy use, but the cerebellum showed less of this reduction. Specifically, Purkinje neurons were strongly associated with genes that, when suppressed by heat, are involved in synaptic function and communication. This work suggests that the unique gene expression patterns in Purkinje neurons may explain their susceptibility to heatstroke and identified a potential drug target to counteract these effects.
