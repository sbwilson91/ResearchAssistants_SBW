### [Emergent topological structure in spontaneous brain-organoid activity](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.17.739228)
**Bodnia, E.; Basart, M.; Hai, S.; Ford, L.; Miolane, N.; Kosik, K. S.; Bouwmeester, D.; Carr, L. D.** | Neuroscience | 2026-11-06 | `organoid`

> This study investigates whether neural activity in organoids exhibits a low-dimensional structure that can be detected by analyzing patterns of correlations. Researchers used a technique called persistent homology to examine recordings from human and mouse cortical organoids. They found that "loops" in the data, representing a specific type of structure, were significantly present in most recordings and remained even when some neurons were removed. The presence of these loops was more sensitive to the removal of specific neurons than random removal, and more complex structures (second homology) appeared in larger networks. This suggests that persistent homology can effectively identify underlying organizational principles in neural recordings at the scale typically obtained in experiments.
