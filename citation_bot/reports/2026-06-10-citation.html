Prenatal body fluid analysis in the evaluation of CAKUT

Thomas Simon, Bénédicte Buffin‐Meyer, Julie Klein et al. | 2026-06-05

Microscopy Transcriptomics

AI Summary: Congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT) are a major cause of chronic kidney disease in children, but current prenatal methods like ultrasound and genetic testing have limited ability to predict postnatal renal outcomes. This review summarizes existing prenatal prognostic strategies and investigates emerging molecular approaches, such as transcriptomics, metabolomics, and proteomics, with a particular emphasis on peptide-based profiling. It highlights that prenatal peptide signatures derived from fetal urine or amniotic fluid show strong promise in predicting postnatal renal outcomes, potentially capturing early molecular processes related to tissue remodeling and inflammation. Despite challenges in clinical translation and validation, integrating molecular peptide profiling with advanced imaging could significantly improve prenatal counseling and clinical decision-making for children with CAKUT.
