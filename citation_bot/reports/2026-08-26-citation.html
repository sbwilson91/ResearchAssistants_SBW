The making of cells and organs in xenogeneic animal bioreactor

Yunhan Tang, Ye Feng, Zhu Chen et al. | 2026-08-24

AI Summary: Organ transplantation is the only viable treatment for end-stage organ dysfunction, but it is severely limited by a critical shortage of donor organs and a lack of technology for long-term organ generation. This review explores the use of xenogeneic animals as "in vivo bioreactors" to generate functional, transplantable cells, tissues, and organs. The primary method discussed is blastocyst complementation, which involves injecting donor pluripotent stem cells into an organogenesis-disabled host embryo to allow the donor cells to form the missing organ. Recent advancements have successfully demonstrated the generation of xenogeneic tissues and organs in rodents and large animals using this technique, along with other methods like organogenic niche replacement and cell expansion in immunodeficient animals. This approach offers a promising solution to the organ shortage, with the review discussing the historical context, efficiency factors, and future prospects, including the potential for generating human blood cells in pigs.

Beyond monolayers: a comparative analysis of 2D cell cultures and 3D in vitro models as new approach methodologies

Italo Rodrigo Calori, Ana Paula Pereira Guimaraes, Ananth Kumar Kammala et al. | 2026-08-24

AI Summary: Traditional 2D cell cultures and animal models face limitations in accurately recapitulating human tissue complexity and predictive capacity, driving the search for more physiologically relevant in vitro systems. This literature review focuses on studies that directly compare various 3D cell culture models, such as spheroids, organoids, and bioprinted models, with their 2D counterparts. The review highlights the potential and limitations of these 3D models as human-relevant New Approach Methodologies (NAMs). Ultimately, these advanced 3D systems offer improved physiological relevance, addressing ethical concerns and limitations associated with animal testing by aligning with the 3Rs principles.

Biomaterials in organoid research: current state and future directions

Laura Klasen, Ramin Nasehi, Lennart Selzener et al. | 2026-08-21

AI Summary: Organoid technology, despite its potential for modeling disease and drug responses, is limited by reproducibility and physiological accuracy challenges stemming from inadequate extracellular matrix-mimicking biomaterials. This mini-review summarizes recent advances in natural, synthetic, and hybrid biomaterials, highlighting engineered hydrogels and synthetic systems with tunable properties like viscoelasticity and degradability. It also discusses emerging trends such as 3D/4D bioprinting, granular hydrogels, organ-on-a-chip platforms, and AI-driven methods that support scalable optimization. These developments signify a crucial transition from generic matrix-based cultures to engineered, tunable, and dynamic microenvironments. Ultimately, advanced biomaterial design is presented as a fundamental element for creating more reproducible and physiologically accurate next-generation organoid systems.
