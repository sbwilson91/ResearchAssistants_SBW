Patterning human kidney organoids with synthetic Wnt-secreting organizers

Connor Fausto, Fokion Glykofrydis, Navneet Kumar et al. | 2026-07-02

Transcriptomics

AI Summary: Human stem cell-derived kidney organoids, while mimicking some developmental aspects, often lack the precise spatial organization found in natural tissues. This study investigated how organizing geometry operates during kidney development and whether it could be reconstructed in vitro. Using spatial transcriptomics of human kidney development, researchers discovered that nascent nephrons establish a specific polarity at a WNT11-WNT9B signaling boundary. They then engineered WNT-secreting cellular organizers and introduced them into kidney organoids, successfully restoring this organizing geometry, biasing distal nephron differentiation, and orienting nephron development. This work demonstrates that synthetic signaling cues can be used to control complex tissue patterning in lab-grown organs.
