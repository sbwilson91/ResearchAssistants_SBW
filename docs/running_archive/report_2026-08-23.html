That stability during a disrupted, lower-volume month is actually a positive signal — fitness hasn't eroded during the taper. The problem is that 55.2 is still short of the ~58 ml/kg/min typically associated with sub-1:30 half marathon performance, and the Garmin HM prediction of 1:33:34 — while 20 seconds faster than it was in late June — still sits 3:34 above target. The honest read: you're in 1:31–1:33 shape right now, not 1:29:59 shape. The margin will depend on ultra recovery in the three weeks between Backyard and Copenhagen.

The HRV status sitting UNBALANCED at 78ms combined with a readiness score of 67/100 is notable. The load ratio of 0.5 means training stress is genuinely low, so this isn't a training-overload signal — it's almost certainly a non-training stressor. The Thursday session, the upcoming race anxiety, or the cycling trip may all be contributing. With readiness at 67, the body is telling you it's not fully recovered from the cumulative block, which is appropriate and expected six days out from a 24-hour ultra. There's nothing here requiring intervention — the physiology is exactly where you'd want it: not stressed by training, but not so flat it's lost sharpness.

The aerobic efficiency reading of 5:40/km at 130–145 bpm is worth marking. That's 17 seconds per kilometre faster than the 8-week average — a meaningful move toward the sub-5:30/km target that was set as the primary aerobic lever for the half marathon block. It suggests the base work through July and early August has landed, and that even during a reduced week, the system is consolidating rather than regressing.

The vertical oscillation average of 9.2cm (92mm) this week remains 1.2cm above the 8.0cm target, and this is the one persistent form issue that hasn't responded to the volume and quality work of the past eight weeks. The mechanism is clear: insufficient posterior chain stiffness means more energy is lost vertically per stride rather than converted into horizontal propulsion. For Backyard Ultra, this matters less — at 6:30–7:00/km easy loops, economy losses are absorbed by low intensity. But for the half marathon, each centimetre of excess oscillation costs pace at 4:16/km, and this is where the S&C work becomes directly relevant to the 20th September target.

With no cadence data extractable from quality efforts this week, there's no regression to flag — but there's also no progress to report. The 2×/week calf and posterior chain work flagged in the nutrition and recovery targets is the mechanism that closes this gap, not pace cues during the race itself. The three-week window between Backyard Ultra and Copenhagen is where consistent S&C execution will make a measurable difference to race-day oscillation.
