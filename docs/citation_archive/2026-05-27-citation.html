Carla Pou Casellas, Fjodor A. Yousef Yengej, Carola M. E. Ammerlaan et al. | 2026-05-23

Transcriptomics

AI Summary: This study aimed to better understand the differentiation and diversity of advanced human *in vitro* kidney models, such as organoids and tubuloids, which are vital for renal research. To achieve this, researchers compared the transcriptomic profiles of kidney tubular epithelial cells from several 3D *in vitro* models, including tissue-derived tubuloids and iPSC-derived organoids, against physiological *in vivo* adult and fetal kidney profiles. Zhili Guo, Zhang Nenghua | 2026-05-20

AI Summary: This article addresses the significant ethical challenges associated with the rapidly advancing organoid co-culture model, a promising tool for biomedical research. It identifies specific ethical dilemmas arising from the cellular sources of organoids, including questions about ongoing donor informed consent, the commercial use of donated materials, and whether organoids possess attributes of "potential life." The authors highlight that the clinical translation of organoid technology pushes existing ethical boundaries, necessitating appropriate governance. They conclude that tailored review guidelines and ethical frameworks are crucial for resolving these issues. This work is significant for ensuring the responsible development and clinical application of organoid technology by proactively addressing its unique ethical landscape.

Disease modelling with in vitro vascularised organoids

Chloe Kan, L Liu, Rio Sugimura | 2026-05-20

AI Summary: This article addresses the crucial need for vascularized organoids to more accurately mimic human tissue microenvironments, given the vital role of blood vessels in health and disease. It reviews and discusses various current approaches for fabricating vasculature within organoids, including co-culture with endothelial cells, assembloids, genetic engineering, and 3D bioprinting. The authors compare the advantages and limitations of these fabrication methods, specifically in their application for disease modeling. Ultimately, the work highlights current challenges and future directions for developing vascularized organoids that can faithfully replicate human physiological conditions and serve as clinically relevant models for human diseases.
