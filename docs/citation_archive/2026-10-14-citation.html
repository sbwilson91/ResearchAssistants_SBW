Kidney Organoids: Emerging Models for Kidney Fibrosis—From Current Advances to Experimental Insights

Lida Chen, Yunzhou Dong, Jingjing Wang et al. | 2026-10-08

AI Summary: Chronic kidney disease frequently progresses to renal fibrosis, a complex pathological process that current animal and 2D cell models struggle to replicate with human relevance. Human pluripotent stem cell-derived kidney organoids have emerged as promising 3D models, partially recapitulating renal development and multicellular composition. These organoids have been successfully applied to model fibrotic remodeling induced by various stressors, enabling the investigation of epithelial damage, fibroblast activation, and therapeutic responses in a human cellular context. However, current organoid models are limited by developmental immaturity and incomplete vascularization. This review summarizes the current state of organoid-based fibrosis modeling and outlines future strategies to enhance their translational value for understanding and treating renal fibrosis.
