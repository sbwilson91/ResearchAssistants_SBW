Organoids in drug discovery and development: emerging technologies to transition beyond animal models

Jolene Phelps, Amanda Orr, Stephanie Michelle Willerth | 2026-10-05

AI Summary: This review addresses the need for improved drug development technologies, driven by high translational failure rates and ethical concerns surrounding animal models, by focusing on the potential of organoids. It evaluates how these human-relevant, 3D stem cell structures, enhanced by technologies like bioprinting and organ-on-chip, can complement or potentially replace animal testing. The review finds that organoids offer significant value across drug development stages, from high-throughput screening to predicting patient-specific responses. However, it also highlights practical challenges in standardization, morphological complexity, heterogeneity, and cellular maturation that currently limit their broad application. Ultimately, organoid technologies represent a strategic shift towards more human-relevant drug discovery, promising increased patient safety, efficiency, and reduced reliance on animal testing.

Tonicity drives collecting duct maturation in the mammalian kidney

Daisuke Inoue, Koichiro Miike, Shunsuke Tanigawa et al. | 2026-10-02

AI Summary: This study investigated whether the high salt environment of the renal medulla, essential for urine concentration, also contributes to kidney maturation. Researchers demonstrated, both in vitro and in vivo, that high tonicity (salt) drives the morphological, transcriptional, and functional maturation of medullary collecting ducts, primarily via nuclear factor of activated T cells 5 (NFAT5). By combining high tonicity with maturation-promoting hormones, they established a robust protocol for maturing human medullary collecting duct organoids and a functional assay to measure water permeability. This platform successfully modeled disease states such as NFAT5 deletion and two forms of nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. These findings establish high tonicity as a key driver of mammalian kidney maturation, offering a promising platform to maximize the potential of organoids for future therapies and disease modeling.

Organoids: Current Applications and Future Directions

Yueqi Leng, Yue Wang, Canhui Cao et al. | 2026-09-30

AI Summary: This review addresses the need for an updated synthesis of organoid technology, acknowledging their potential in studying human development and disease but also their current limitations, such as incomplete maturation and insufficient vascularization. It provides a comprehensive overview of organoid generation strategies, covering cell sources, 3D culture systems, and microenvironment engineering, alongside representative applications across various organ types. The authors discuss current technical bottlenecks and highlight emerging solutions, including organ-on-chip platforms, multiomics, CRISPR-based engineering, and artificial intelligence. Ultimately, this work aims to provide a framework for developing more physiologically relevant and clinically translatable organoid models.
