Single-cell spatial mapping of human kidney development implicates the microenvironment in guiding cell fate decisions

Jonathan Levinsohn, Samuel Grindel, Bernhard Dumoulin et al. | 2026-07-30

Transcriptomics

AI Summary: This study investigated how cell-cell communication coordinates human kidney development, addressing the limitations of studying single interactions in model organisms. Researchers employed single-cell RNA sequencing and spatial transcriptomics on over 700,000 cells to map gene expression and differentiation trajectories in space. Their analysis defined the spatial organization of kidney development and revealed previously unrecognized cell fate plasticity, demonstrating that early patterning decisions can be revised. Furthermore, a genome-wide, spatially aware cell-cell interaction analysis linked localized ligand signals to cell fate decisions. This work provides a potential mechanism for robust cell fate establishment in complex tissues and offers a blueprint for understanding human development coordination at scale.
