Engineering colorectal cancer models across the primary tumor–portal circulation–liver metastasis continuum: Organoids, 3D bioprinting, and microphysiological systems

Chengyao Jiang, Xuan Zhang, Sheng Zhang et al. | 2026-11-12

AI Summary: This review addresses the challenge of creating accurate colorectal cancer (CRC) models that fully replicate the complex tumor microenvironment and the entire metastatic process, especially the spread to the liver. It systematically analyzes a range of engineered CRC models, from organoids to 3D-bioprinted constructs and microphysiological systems, focusing on their capacity to simulate the primary tumor-to-liver metastasis continuum. The review finds that while newer models are improving by incorporating elements like organ-specific matrices, immune cells, and vascularization to study key metastatic steps, most still only capture parts of the process, and clinical validation remains limited. To guide future development, the authors propose frameworks for assessing model fidelity and a minimum reporting checklist. Ultimately, they suggest that combining functional, molecular, spatial, and clinical data could enhance the translational relevance of these models, though their prospective clinical utility still needs to be established.
