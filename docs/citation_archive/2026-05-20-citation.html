Next-generation 3D bioprinted inner ear organoids

Xingyu Chen, Minghong He, Yuexin He et al. | 2026-05-15

AI Summary: This abstract addresses the critical need for more effective treatments for irreversible inner ear damage, highlighting that current inner ear organoid models lack the morphological fidelity required to accurately recapitulate normal physiology. To overcome this limitation, the authors propose leveraging advanced 3D bioprinting technology, which enables the high-fidelity construction of inner ear organoids through the precise spatial assembly of cell-laden bioinks. The article explores this connection, outlining a specific 3D bioprinting strategy for creating more biomimetic inner ear models. This approach is envisioned to be crucial for understanding physiological mechanisms, facilitating high-throughput screening, and ultimately developing fundamental new therapies for inner ear diseases.

Bridging Kidney Organoid Innovation and Regenerative Medicine: Current Advances and Future Directions

Bohong Guo, J K Zhang, Xuening Fang et al. | 2026-05-15

Transcriptomics

AI Summary: Chronic kidney disease (CKD) and a shortage of organ donors underscore the urgent need for alternative therapies, making kidney organoids a promising platform for modeling renal development, investigating disease, and exploring regenerative medicine applications. This review summarizes current approaches to generating kidney organoids, detailing differentiation methods and the underlying signaling and epigenetic mechanisms, alongside the contributions of bioengineering and single-cell transcriptomics. Despite their potential, current organoids are limited by immature tissue architecture, insufficient vascularization, and unaddressed safety concerns, which impede their clinical translation. The authors argue that existing optimization frameworks are inadequate, proposing a paradigm shift that integrates bioengineering modulation with high-resolution functional assessment to advance organoid complexity and physiological utility for future therapeutic use.
