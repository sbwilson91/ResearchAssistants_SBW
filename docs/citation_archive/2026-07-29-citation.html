Post-translational modification of proteins in the human testis development pathway

Firman P Idris, Lucas G. A. Ferreira, Brendan J. Houston et al. | 2026-07-23

Transcriptomics

AI Summary: This review addresses the significant knowledge gap regarding the role and regulation of protein post-translational modifications (PTMs) during human foetal testis development, particularly their involvement in normal development and differences of sex development (DSD). The authors conducted a comprehensive literature review, synthesizing information from various databases to identify validated PTMs on proteins critical for testicular development. They found that proteins essential for human foetal testis development are extensively modified by PTMs, and disruptions in specific PTM sites or pathways are linked to DSD. Despite technical and ethical challenges in studying human foetal gonads, emerging high-sensitivity proteomics and stem cell models offer promising avenues for future research. Understanding PTMs in testis development is crucial for improving genetic diagnoses of DSD, elucidating how environmental factors affect testicular function, and addressing broader concerns about declining global fertility rates.
