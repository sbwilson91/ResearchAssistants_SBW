Biomaterials in 3D bioprinting: Design strategies, material-technique interactions, and biomedical applications

Ghaida Harb, Maha Abumousa, Monther Al-Khateeb et al. | 2026-11-07

AI Summary: This review critically synthesizes the role of biomaterials in 3D bioprinting by examining how material properties, fabrication techniques, and biological requirements interact to determine construct performance across various biomedical applications. Unlike other reviews, it specifically focuses on these coupled interactions across multiple tissue systems. The analysis reveals that no single biomaterial satisfies all design requirements, leading to necessary compromises between mechanical strength, printability, and biological performance, with specific material choices depending on the tissue type. Printing techniques also impose constraints, driving the use of composite biomaterials and hybrid fabrication strategies. Despite advances, challenges like mechanical mismatch and limited vascularization remain, with 4D printing, smart biomaterials, and AI-assisted design highlighted as emerging strategies to advance clinically translatable bioprinted constructs.

Co-culture with embryonic renal stromal progenitor cell enhances podocyte differentiation and modulates doxorubicin-induced injury responses

Tien-Hsiang Tang, Kotaro Doi, Yasuyuki Sakai et al. | 2026-11-06

AI Summary: This study investigated whether co-culturing podocytes with renal stromal progenitor cells (rSPCs) could create a more supportive microenvironment to enhance podocyte differentiation in vitro. Researchers cultured mouse podocytes alone or in combination with embryonic rSPCs on porous membranes, then assessed podocyte marker expression, morphology, and function. They found that co-culture with rSPCs, especially on membranes with larger pores, significantly improved podocyte differentiation and promoted a more mature, podocyte-like morphology. This co-culture model also maintained size-selective permeability and showed a more physiological, dose-dependent response to toxic injury. These findings suggest that rSPCs provide crucial developmental cues, offering a more physiologically relevant in vitro model for studying glomerular development and kidney disease.
