Inferring brain structural complexity from dynamical measures

Gaglioti, G.; Cattani, A.; Nieus, T.; Momi, D.; Comolatti, R.; Dalla Porta, L.; Sarasso, S.; Massimini, M. | Neuroscience | 2026-10-26 | organoid

This study investigated how different ways of measuring "neural complexity" relate to the actual structure of brain networks. Researchers used computer simulations of brain activity, systematically changing the connections between simulated neurons to create various network structures. They found that measures of complexity based on observing spontaneous brain activity were inconsistent, with some reflecting network structure better than others. However, measures of complexity based on how the network responds to external stimuli were more reliable, consistently showing the highest values in networks with a balance of local and widespread connections, similar to a waking brain state. This suggests that perturbational complexity measures may be more useful for understanding brain architecture in real-world research and clinical applications.
