This study investigated whether high levels of 25-hydroxycholesterol (25-HC), a substance produced by immune cells, make kidneys more vulnerable to statin-induced damage. Researchers found that statins caused kidney cell death when combined with 25-HC, and this was also observed in mice. The damage occurred because 25-HC interfered with a cellular cleaning process called autophagy, which could be reversed by adding a substance that restores normal cell function or by reducing the immune cells that produce 25-HC. This study investigated how different ways of measuring neural complexity relate to the underlying structure of brain networks. Researchers used computer simulations of neural activity, systematically altering the network's connections to create varying degrees of integration and differentiation. They found that measures of complexity based on spontaneous activity were inconsistent, reflecting different aspects of the network structure. However, measures based on how the network responds to external stimuli consistently showed the highest complexity in networks with a balance of local and widespread connections, similar to a waking brain state.
