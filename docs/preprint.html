This research addresses the challenge of comparing different human kidney organoid models by analyzing their cellular development. Scientists used single-cell and single-nucleus transcriptomes from 11 studies, comparing them to fetal and adult kidney samples. Lineage tracing in rhabdoid tumor organoids and orthotopic xenografts reveals convergent transcriptional states

This study investigated whether the diverse gene activity seen in aggressive pediatric tumors called malignant rhabdoid tumors (MRTs) arises from different tumor cell types or from cells changing their behavior. Researchers used a technique to track cell lineages and single-cell RNA sequencing in lab-grown MRT models and in animal studies. They found that tumors and cultures narrowed down to a few dominant cell lineages, and these expanding lineages had distinct gene expression patterns. Importantly, different lineages could adopt similar gene expression patterns, and these patterns were consistent even when the tumors spread, suggesting a limited set of available states. The research indicates that while the specific outcome of which cell types become dominant can vary, the range of gene expression patterns available to these tumors is restricted and recurring.

Emergent topological structure in spontaneous brain-organoid activity

Bodnia, E.; Basart, M.; Hai, S.; Ford, L.; Miolane, N.; Kosik, K. S.; Bouwmeester, D.; Carr, L. D. | Neuroscience | 2026-11-06 | organoid

This study investigates whether neural activity in organoid brain models exhibits a low-dimensional structure, even when analyzed from complex, high-dimensional data. Researchers used a mathematical technique called persistent homology to analyze patterns of electrical activity recorded from human and mouse brain organoids. They discovered that the activity patterns often contained "loops" (a measure of topological structure) that were unlikely to occur by chance, and this structure remained even when some recorded neurons were removed. The findings suggest that persistent homology can effectively reveal underlying organizational principles in neural recordings at the scale typically obtained in experiments.
