Bx42 directs neural stem cell exit from quiescence through Prospero

Losurdo, N.; Mgbike, U. E.; Dietze, M.; Hixson, I. J.; Bibo, A.; Mao, X.; Link, N. | Developmental Biology | 2026-08-12 | organoid

This study investigated how genetic mutations cause microcephaly, a condition where a child's head and brain are significantly smaller than normal. Researchers found that a gene called Bx42 is crucial for this process, using studies in human brain organoids and fruit flies. They discovered that when Bx42 is absent, neural stem cells don't divide enough early in development, leading to a smaller brain. This happens because Bx42 helps these stem cells exit a resting state and start multiplying, a process influenced by another factor called Prospero. The findings highlight a new pathway involved in brain development and offer insights into the causes of microcephaly linked to specific genetic changes.

The Social Cognition Paradox in Long-Duration Spaceflight: A VEN Fatigue Hypothesis for Duration-Dependent Emotion Recognition Decline

Keskin, E.; McNerney, M. W.; Ali, N. | Neuroscience | 2026-08-12 | organoid

This research investigates why long space missions might negatively impact social cognition, a phenomenon not fully understood. Scientists examined this by integrating data from various studies, including experiments with rodents, human brain organoids, astronaut blood samples, and brain scans. They found evidence suggesting that the brain's social processing networks, particularly those involving Von Economo Neurons (VENs), undergo adaptive changes like increased myelination during shorter spaceflights. However, these compensatory mechanisms appear to fail during very long missions, leading to a decline in social cognition. This work proposes that VEN fatigue and associated brain network remodeling are key biological explanations for these duration-dependent effects on social cognition in space.

Asymmetric cortical mechanics shape epithelial cell geometry in organoids

Guyomar, T.; Lu, L.; Hiraiwa, T.; Riveline, D. | Biophysics | 2026-08-12 | organoid

This research investigates how the internal structure of cells influences their shape in 3D tissues. Scientists studied the distribution and mechanical properties of actin and myosin, key components of the cell's internal scaffolding, in organoids and cysts. They then used this data to develop and calibrate a new computational model that combines active fluid theory with a phase field approach. Zhubanchaliyev, A.; Najm, M.; Laigle, V.; Bonnet, E.; Martignetti, L. | Systems Biology | 2026-08-10 | organoid

This study addresses the challenge of analyzing gene activity within biological pathways from single-cell data, aiming to improve interpretability and account for technical variations. The researchers developed scROMA, a method that uses singular-value decomposition to measure pathway activity and provides significance scores.
