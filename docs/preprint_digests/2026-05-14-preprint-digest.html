This study addressed the challenge of investigating tumor-associated macrophages (TAM) within a native human tumor microenvironment (TME) due to a lack of suitable model systems. Researchers developed air-liquid interface (ALI)-grown patient-derived organoids (PDOs) that successfully preserved TAM, stroma, and immune subsets, maintaining their function and responsiveness. InterScale reveals multi-scale cellular interaction programs in spatial transcriptomics

This study addresses the challenge of comprehensively analyzing cell-cell interactions across both local and long-range spatial scales using spatial transcriptomics data, as existing methods often capture only one type of interaction. To overcome this, researchers developed InterScale, a novel graph-transformer computational approach that integrates a Graph Convolutional Network for local interactions with a global transformer encoder to jointly model multi-scale cellular communication. Applied to neural organoids, InterScale successfully resolved spatially restricted neuronal differentiation programs and broader progenitor states along a morphogen gradient. Additionally, in human pancreatic tissue, it revealed disease-associated spatial reorganization and tissue remodeling in type 1 diabetes. This modular and scalable framework provides an unbiased and biologically interpretable tool for studying complex cellular interactions across diverse spatial transcriptomics platforms, offering valuable insights into tissue homeostasis and disease.
