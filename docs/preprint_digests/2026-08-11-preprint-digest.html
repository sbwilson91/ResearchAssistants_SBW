Plaque-associated oligodendrocyte proteostatic failure underlies myelin loss in Alzheimers disease

This research investigates why myelin, crucial for brain cell communication, is lost early in Alzheimer's disease. Scientists used human stem cells to create brain organoids that could produce mature myelin-producing cells. When they introduced Alzheimer's-related mutations into these organoids, they observed myelin damage and identified that oligodendrocytes near amyloid plaques showed problems with calcium signaling, immune responses, and breaking down proteins. The study found that myelin protein levels decreased because the proteins were being degraded, not due to a lack of production, and this degradation could be reversed by blocking protein breakdown. These findings suggest that a failure in protein management within oligodendrocytes, triggered by amyloid plaques, is a key reason for myelin loss in Alzheimer's and could be a target for future treatments.

Zhubanchaliyev, A.; Najm, M.; Laigle, V.; Bonnet, E.; Martignetti, L. | Systems Biology | 2026-08-10 | organoid

This study addresses the need for better methods to analyze gene activity in single-cell data, specifically focusing on functional pathways. The researchers developed scROMA, a new method that uses singular value decomposition to measure pathway activity and provides significance scores. A key innovation is a batch-aware version of scROMA that corrects for technical variations at the pathway level, which is shown to be more effective than methods correcting the entire dataset. The work also includes a simulation tool for testing pathway analysis methods and demonstrates scROMA's ability to identify biological signals in real-world datasets while separating them from technical noise.
