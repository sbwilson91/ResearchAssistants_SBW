Droplet-based organoid screens enable high-throughput identification of cancer vulnerabilities

This research addresses the challenge of scaling up patient-derived cancer organoid cultures for precision oncology. The scientists developed a new method called "droplet culture" where tumor cells are grown in tiny, nanoliter-sized compartments within Matrigel droplets, allowing for high-throughput culturing. This approach enabled them to screen thousands of drugs and perform genetic screens, revealing new therapeutic possibilities and an unexpected vulnerability in stomach cancer related to nucleotide-sugar balance. The droplet culture technology offers a versatile and scalable way to discover new cancer treatments.

This study investigated how cabozantinib affects the tumor microenvironment in pancreatic cancer, which is a very deadly disease with poor survival rates. Researchers used advanced imaging techniques to analyze patient tumor samples and created lab-grown models of the tumors with their surrounding cells. They found that cabozantinib, especially when combined with another drug, reduced the number of cells that suppress the immune system and increased cancer cell death. This suggests that cabozantinib could be a valuable treatment option for pancreatic cancer by overcoming resistance mechanisms in the tumor's environment.
