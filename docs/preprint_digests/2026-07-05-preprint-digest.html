Colorectal cancers with distinct metastatic potential trigger divergent early T cell responses

Saad, M.; Thoms, A.; Yin, Y. S.; Mamede, I.; Rahman, A.; Chen, R.; Carrasco, S. E.; Darcy, P. W.; Goto, N.; Tavazoie, S.... | Immunology | 2026-07-04 | organoid

This study investigated how T cell responses differ between non-metastatic and pro-metastatic colorectal cancers. Researchers used transplanted CRC organoids and tracked T cell behavior over time. They found that non-metastatic tumors attracted T cells that became active and capable of killing cancer cells, while pro-metastatic tumors resulted in T cells that remained largely inactive. Importantly, T cells interacting directly with non-metastatic tumor cells were more active and cytotoxic, and these T cells helped slow tumor growth. The findings suggest that a failure of the immune system to effectively engage with early tumors contributes to their ability to spread.
