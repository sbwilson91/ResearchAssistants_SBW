Evaluation design shapes estimates of cross-donor generalization in single-cell classification of cell-type

Chen, X.; Li, J.; Battle, A. | Bioinformatics | 2026-11-10 | scRNA

This study investigated how different ways of testing cell-type classifiers affect their ability to generalize to new individuals. The researchers compared using random cell splits versus holding out entire donors for testing. They found that random cell splits consistently overestimated classifier performance, especially for more complex datasets. This overestimation was not resolved by accounting for technical variations or using advanced statistical models. The findings highlight that testing with held-out donors is crucial for accurately assessing how well a classifier will work on new, unseen individuals, particularly when the classification task is challenging.

PRISM: probabilistic cell-type resolution with integrated spatial modelling for simultaneous ambient RNA decontamination and doublet correction in spatial transcriptomics

Cupaioli, F.; Merelli, I. | Bioinformatics | 2026-11-09 | spatial transcriptomics

This study addresses the problem of inaccurate spatial transcriptomics data caused by ambient RNA contamination and cell doublets. The researchers developed a new computational framework called PRISM that simultaneously identifies cell types, detects doublets, and estimates ambient RNA contamination for each cell. PRISM was shown to accurately recover simulated data and, when applied to real mouse brain data, it identified a significant number of doublets, corrected misleading signals related to myelin, and improved the purity of oligodendrocyte data. This work provides a more accurate way to analyze spatial transcriptomics data, leading to better biological insights.
