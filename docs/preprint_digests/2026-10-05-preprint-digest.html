This research investigates how nerve cells called interneurons migrate into the human brain after birth, a process that cannot be studied in living rodents. Scientists used long-term human brain organoids, grown for over six months, to observe this migration. They found that interneurons move in chains, and that other cells called astrocytes help organize these chains into streams. The study also identified specific genes active in these migratory streams, some of which are linked to neurodevelopmental disorders like autism. This research investigated how different human tissues respond to a lack of mechanical load, mimicking conditions in space. Scientists used rotating-wall vessels to simulate microgravity for 24 hours on neural, cardiac, and tonsil organoids. They found that these organoids showed tissue-specific gene expression changes similar to those observed in actual spaceflight, with neural and cardiac organoids appearing "older" transcriptomically.
