This study investigated how different levels of a protein called GFAP, both normal and mutated, affect cells. Researchers used a technique called single-cell proteomics to analyze the proteins within individual cells. They found that high levels of normal GFAP caused proteomic changes similar to those seen with a specific mutation linked to Alexander disease.
