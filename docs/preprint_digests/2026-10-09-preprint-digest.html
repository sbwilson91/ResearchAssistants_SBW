Glia-enriched human brain organoids for studying developmental alterations in leukodystrophies

Mangiameli, E.; Kok, L. M. L.; Rosato, C.; Cenini, G.; Cupaioli, F. A.; Alfieri, R.; Vitchanthangal Prathivathibayankara... | Neuroscience | 2026-10-08 | organoid

This research investigates the underlying causes and developmental origins of leukodystrophies, a group of inherited white matter disorders. The scientists used human induced pluripotent stem cells to create 3D neural organoids, specifically adapted to mimic later, glia-rich developmental stages relevant to these diseases. They found that these organoids, when modeling specific leukodystrophies like globoid cell leukodystrophy, showed shared and distinct changes in gene activity related to cellular processes like metabolism and stress response. This work provides a new way to study these complex brain disorders by using human-derived models that better represent disease-relevant developmental stages.

Transient Vascular Cell Integration and Intrinsic Media-Stimulated Endothelial Activation Reveal Diabetic Retinopathy Signatures in Human Retinal Organoids

Tsikandelova, R.; Schettler, R.; Teasdale, M. D.; Kelk, J.; Boczonadi, V.; Dorgau, B.; Hussain, R.; Coxhead, J. M.; Medi... | Neuroscience | 2026-10-08 | organoid

This research aimed to improve retinal organoids by adding blood vessels, which are missing in current models and limit their use for studying eye diseases. The scientists introduced vascular organoid cells into developing retinal organoids and cultured them for an extended period. They found that these vascular cells could integrate without harming the retinal structure or function, and even protected it from damage caused by pro-angiogenic conditions. This work suggests a way to create more complete retinal models that could be useful for understanding and treating diseases like diabetic retinopathy.

Connectomics by Sequencing Reveals Self-Organizing Principles of Neuronal Networks in Cerebral Organoids

Vertesy, A.; Najm, R.; Doleschall, B.; Isaev, S.; Zabolocki, M.; Novatchkova, M.; Burkard, T. R.; Ziegler, C.; Krautberg... | Neuroscience | 2026-10-08 | organoid

This study investigated how neural connections form and are altered in disease by linking individual neuron connections to their molecular identity. Researchers developed a new method called COSCO, which combines tracing neural connections with sequencing RNA from single cells in brain organoids. They found that these organoid networks organize into predictable patterns based on internal cell rules, similar to mouse brains, and that these wiring patterns are related to specific gene activity, particularly those involved in cell adhesion and synapses. Importantly, in a model of Tuberous Sclerosis Complex, abnormal brain activity was linked to changes in network structure, suggesting that this method can be used to study how human brain connections develop and are disrupted in neurodevelopmental disorders.
